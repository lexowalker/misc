# I'll Never Love Again: lyrics

B minor, 66 BPM, 4/4, 4:40. Times are from `ill-never-love-again.mid`.

## Verse 1 (0:14)

Your cup is still up on the shelf.
I wash it sometimes. God knows why.
I leave the hall light on all night
the way I did when you'd come by.

## Chorus (0:43)

They ask me and I say I'm fine.
I've said it till it's nearly true:
I'll never love again.
I'd have to finish loving you.

## How the words work

**The engine.** The title is a vow that the song disproves. Each section
disproves it a little more openly:

| Section | What the singer says | What the listener hears |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | Small habits. A cup, a light. | He still keeps house for two. |
| Chorus | "I'm fine." Then the vow. Then one more line. | The last line cancels the vow: he cannot love again because he has not finished. |
| Verse 2 | Someone new and kind. | The body moves on; the cup goes back on the shelf. |
| Bridge | He chooses the ache. | The pain is the last of the love, so he keeps it. |
| Solo | No words. | The guitar says the part he cannot. |
| Coda | The light, the vow, three words. | The vow was true all the time, for the opposite reason. |

**Two images, both changed by the end.**

- *The cup.* Verse 1: it is on the shelf. Verse 2: someone moves it and he
  puts it back. One small domestic object carries the whole second verse.
- *The hall light.* Verse 1: he leaves it on from habit, "the way I did when
  you'd come by". Coda: "I let it burn". The same light, now a choice.

**The last line.** Short, absolute, quiet: "I never stopped." It is three
syllables on three low notes, and the last one is the tonic.

## How the words sit on the music

- Verse lines are eight syllables in iambic rhythm (da-DUM four times). Each
  line takes two bars. The stressed second and eighth syllables land on beat 3,
  on a chord tone. The verse melody climbs line by line while the bass falls.
- "I'll never love again" is the highest phrase in the chorus. "Love" is the
  top note (C#5), a leap of a fifth, held. "Again" lands on Gmaj7, not on the
  home chord: the harmony does not agree with the vow.
- "I'd have to finish loving you" comes next, low and almost spoken. It walks
  down to the tonic. This is the only line in the chorus that resolves.
- The bridge is the highest and most sustained vocal part ("ache" is held).
  It ends unresolved on the dominant, and the solo answers it.
- In the coda the guitar holds its last note (the tonic, bent up to B5) under
  "The light's still on". The hook comes one more time, and lands on Gmaj7 one
  more time. "I never stopped" then resolves alone.
- The MIDI file carries the lyric as lyric events, one syllable per note, on
  the guide vocal track.

## What comes from the references

Only moves and devices, no lines. See `../lyric-inspiration.md`.

| Device | Where we use it |
|---|---|
| A declaration that undoes itself (Neruda, Poem 20) | The chorus: vow, then "I'd have to finish loving you". |
| Plain, direct speech; let the guitar be the poetry (Moore) | Every verse line is ordinary talk. No metaphor until the bridge. |
| Sung with a new, kind person in the room (Cat Stevens) | Verse 2. |
| The small domestic detail that wounds (Tolstoy) | The cup. |
| One image that changes between the first verse and the end (Tolstoy's oak) | The hall light. |
| The body moves on, the heart does not (Márquez) | Verse 2. |
| The wound you would not heal (St John of the Cross) | The bridge. |
| The last brightening before the dark (Tolstoy's candle) | "I let it burn", and the solo's last climb. |
| A short, absolute last line (St John, "y eras ido") | "I never stopped." |
| A vow kept by choice (Márquez's Amaranta) | The coda: the vow is now chosen. |

## Open choices

- **Who the chorus speaks to.** Now "they" ask and he answers; the last line
  turns to "you". If you want the whole chorus to address her, line 1 can be
  "You'd ask me and I'd say I'm fine."
- **"God knows why."** A softer option: "I wash it sometimes. Don't know why."
- **Gender.** Verse 2 says "she". Nothing else in the lyric fixes gender.
