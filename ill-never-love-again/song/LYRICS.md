# I'll Never Love Again: lyrics

B minor, 66 BPM, 4/4, 4:40. Times are from `ill-never-love-again.mid`.

## Verse 1 (0:14)

You laughed before the joke was done.
You sang off-key and sang it loud.
You never closed a cupboard door.
The house is very tidy now.

## Chorus (0:43)

So here it is. It isn't new,
and all of it is nearly true:
I'll never love again.
I'd have to finish loving you.

*Guitar: the theme, two bars (1:05)*

## Verse 2 (1:12)

There's someone kind who stays some nights.
She waits until the joke is through.
She closes every cupboard door.
She's lovely, and she isn't you.

## Chorus (1:41)

So here it is. It isn't new,
and all of it is nearly true:
I'll never love again.
I'd have to finish loving you.

## Bridge (2:03)

Time's meant to take the edge off this.
Let time, for once, be wrong.
The day it doesn't hurt at all
is when you're really gone.

## Guitar solo (2:32 to 4:14)

Slow theme (2:32), build (3:01), fast (3:23), theme and speed together (3:45).

## Coda (4:14)

One cupboard door stays open now.
I'll never love again.
I never stopped.

---

## Rules for this lyric

- **Look at her, not at him.** The verses describe what she did and what the
  new person does. The singer is almost absent. "I" appears only in the hook,
  the line after it, and the last line.
- **Say facts, not feelings.** A feeling is named once ("hurt", in the bridge).
- **Plain speech.** Every line is something a person can say out loud.
- **No stock images.** No rain, ghosts, shadows, flames, ashes, echoes,
  broken hearts, lights left on, empty beds or keepsakes.
- **No explaining.** "The house is very tidy now" does not say why that is sad.
- **The guitar is the poetry.** The words stay small so that the solo can be
  large.

## How the words work

The title is a vow that the song disproves. Each section disproves it a little
more openly:

| Section | What is said | What the listener hears |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | Three things she did. Then the house, now. | She was noise and mess and life. Order is what her absence looks like. |
| Chorus | An old statement, "nearly true". The vow. One more line. | The last line cancels the vow: he cannot love again because he has not finished. |
| Verse 2 | A kind person who does everything correctly. | Each correct thing is one of the three, turned around. She cannot win. |
| Bridge | Time should help. Let it fail. | The pain is the last of her, so he keeps it. |
| Solo | No words. | The guitar says the part he cannot. |
| Coda | One door, open. The vow. Three words. | He keeps one small disorder on purpose. The vow was true for the opposite reason. |

**The verses mirror each other.** Verse 1: she laughed early, sang loud, left
doors open. Verse 2: the new person waits for the joke to end and closes every
door. Nothing is wrong with her. That is the point of "She's lovely, and she
isn't you."

**One detail changes by the end.** The cupboard door. Verse 1: she never
closed one, and now the house is tidy. Verse 2: someone closes all of them.
Coda: one stays open.

**The last line.** "I never stopped." Three low notes; the last one is the tonic.

## How the words sit on the music

- Verse lines are eight syllables in iambic rhythm (da-DUM four times). Each
  line takes two bars. The stressed second and eighth syllables land on beat 3,
  on a chord tone. The verse melody climbs line by line while the bass falls.
- "I'll never love again" is the highest phrase in the chorus. "Love" is the
  top note (C#5), a leap of a fifth, held. "Again" lands on Gmaj7, not on the
  home chord: the harmony does not agree with the vow.
- "I'd have to finish loving you" comes next, low and almost spoken. It walks
  down to the tonic. This is the only line in the chorus that resolves.
- In the bridge, "time" is the long held note. The last word, "gone", rises
  and stays unresolved on the dominant. The solo answers it.
- In the coda the guitar holds its last note (the tonic, bent up to B5) under
  "One cupboard door stays open now". The hook comes one more time, and lands
  on Gmaj7 one more time. "I never stopped" then resolves alone.
- The MIDI file carries the lyric as lyric events, one syllable per note, on
  the guide vocal track. The melody is the same in every draft.

## What comes from the references

Only moves and devices, no lines. See `../lyric-inspiration.md`.

| Device | Where we use it |
|---|---|
| A declaration that undoes itself (Neruda, Poem 20) | The chorus: vow, then "I'd have to finish loving you". |
| Plain, direct speech; let the guitar be the poetry (Moore) | The whole lyric. |
| Then against now | Verse 1: three lines of "you" in the past, one line of now. |
| Sung with a new, kind person in the room (Cat Stevens) | Verse 2. |
| The small domestic detail that wounds (Tolstoy) | The cupboard door. |
| One detail that changes between the first verse and the end (Tolstoy's oak) | The door: open, closed, open again. |
| The wound you would not heal (St John of the Cross) | The bridge. |
| A short, absolute last line (St John, "y eras ido") | "I never stopped." |
| A vow kept by choice (Márquez's Amaranta) | The coda. |

## Revision history

- **Draft 3.** Removed the cup, and nearly all of "I" and "they". The verses
  now describe her and the new person. New chorus lead-in, new first half of
  the bridge, new coda line. The hook, "I'd have to finish loving you",
  the second half of the bridge and "I never stopped" are unchanged.
- **Draft 2.** Removed the hall light, "God knows why", "I say I'm fine",
  "She's good to me" and the abstract bridge. Added the "we" slip.

## Open choices

- **Gender.** Verse 2 says "she". Nothing else in the lyric fixes gender.
- **The coda line.** "One cupboard door stays open now" is an object again,
  but it is her habit and not a keepsake. A version with no object:
  "Nobody here laughs early now."
