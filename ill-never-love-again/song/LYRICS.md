# I'll Never Love Again: lyrics

B minor, 66 BPM, 4/4, 4:40. Times are from `ill-never-love-again.mid`.

## Verse 1 (0:14)

Your cup is still up on the shelf.
It's yours. I drink from something else.
When I mean "I", I still say "we".
My friends have stopped correcting me.

## Chorus (0:43)

They tell me that it's time I tried.
I tell them what is nearly true:
I'll never love again.
I'd have to finish loving you.

*Guitar: the theme, two bars (1:05)*

## Verse 2 (1:12)

There's someone kind who stays some nights.
She says I'm somewhere else. She's right.
Last week she put your cup away.
It's on the shelf again today.

## Chorus (1:41)

They tell me that it's time I tried.
I tell them what is nearly true:
I'll never love again.
I'd have to finish loving you.

## Bridge (2:03)

I'm told that it gets easier.
I hope that it does not.
The day it doesn't hurt at all
is when you're really gone.

## Guitar solo (2:32 to 4:14)

Slow theme (2:32), build (3:01), fast (3:23), theme and speed together (3:45).

## Coda (4:14)

I still say "we". I mean it now.
I'll never love again.
I never stopped.

## Rules for this lyric

- **Say facts, not feelings.** The singer reports what he does: where the cup
  is, which word he uses, what his friends do. He names a feeling only once
  ("hurt", in the bridge). The listener does the rest.
- **Plain speech.** Every line is something a person can say at a kitchen
  table. No line needs a second reading.
- **No stock images.** No rain, ghosts, shadows, flames, ashes, echoes,
  broken or shattered hearts, lights left on, or empty beds.
- **No explaining.** Verse 2 does not say who put the cup back.
- **The guitar is the poetry.** The words stay small so that the solo can be
  large.

## How the words work

The title is a vow that the song disproves. Each section disproves it a little
more openly:

| Section | What the singer says | What the listener hears |
|---|---|---|
| Verse 1 | A cup he does not use. A word he gets wrong. | He still lives as two people. Enough time has passed that his friends gave up. |
| Chorus | People tell him to try again. He gives the vow. Then one more line. | The last line cancels the vow: he cannot love again because he has not finished. |
| Verse 2 | Someone new and kind. She sees it. | The cup goes back on the shelf, and he does not say how. |
| Bridge | He hopes it does not get easier. | The pain is the last of her, so he keeps it. |
| Solo | No words. | The guitar says the part he cannot. |
| Coda | The same word, now on purpose. The vow. Three words. | The vow was true all the time, for the opposite reason. |

**Two details, both changed by the end.**

- *The cup.* Verse 1: it is on the shelf and he does not drink from it.
  Verse 2: she puts it away, and it is on the shelf again.
- *"We".* Verse 1: a slip that his friends no longer correct. Coda: "I mean it
  now". The same word, now a choice.

**The last line.** "I never stopped." Three low notes; the last one is the tonic.

## How the words sit on the music

- Verse lines are eight syllables in iambic rhythm (da-DUM four times). Each
  line takes two bars. The stressed second and eighth syllables land on beat 3,
  on a chord tone. The verse melody climbs line by line while the bass falls.
- "I'll never love again" is the highest phrase in the chorus. "Love" is the
  top note (C#5), a leap of a fifth, held. "Again" lands on Gmaj7, not on the
  home chord: the harmony does not agree with the vow.
- "I'd have to finish loving you" comes next, low and almost spoken. It walks
  down to the tonic. This is the only line in the chorus that resolves.
- In the bridge, "hope" is the long held note. The last word, "gone", rises
  and stays unresolved on the dominant. The solo answers it.
- In the coda the guitar holds its last note (the tonic, bent up to B5) under
  "I still say 'we'". The hook comes one more time, and lands on Gmaj7 one
  more time. "I never stopped" then resolves alone.
- The MIDI file carries the lyric as lyric events, one syllable per note, on
  the guide vocal track. The melody did not change in this revision.

## What comes from the references

Only moves and devices, no lines. See `../lyric-inspiration.md`.

| Device | Where we use it |
|---|---|
| A declaration that undoes itself (Neruda, Poem 20) | The chorus: vow, then "I'd have to finish loving you". |
| Plain, direct speech; let the guitar be the poetry (Moore) | The whole lyric. |
| Sung with a new, kind person in the room (Cat Stevens) | Verse 2. |
| The small domestic detail that wounds (Tolstoy) | The cup; the word "we". |
| One detail that changes between the first verse and the end (Tolstoy's oak) | "We": a slip, then a choice. |
| The body moves on, the heart does not (Márquez) | Verse 2. |
| The wound you would not heal (St John of the Cross) | The bridge. |
| A short, absolute last line (St John, "y eras ido") | "I never stopped." |
| A vow kept by choice (Márquez's Amaranta) | The coda. |

## Revision history

- **Draft 2.** Removed the hall light (a stock image), "God knows why",
  "I say I'm fine", "She's good to me" and the abstract bridge ("ache",
  "healed"). Added the "we" slip and the friends who stopped correcting it.
  The hook, "I'd have to finish loving you" and "I never stopped" are unchanged.

## Open choices

- **Gender.** Verse 2 says "she". Nothing else in the lyric fixes gender.
- **"I'm told that it gets easier."** This is the one borrowed phrase, used so
  that the next line can refuse it. A less familiar option: "They say it
  stops. One day it stops."
