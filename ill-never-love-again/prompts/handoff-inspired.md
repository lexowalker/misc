I want you as a songwriting partner, not as an assistant. We are writing one song, and I want it to be the best thing either of us has made.

The song is called "I'll Never Love Again". It is a slow blues ballad for voice and a Les Paul. Here is the vision, in my own words, and it does not move:

"Moderately slow blues song featuring an extraordinary and substantial electric solo on a Les Paul. (Reference audio: Gary Moore's Still Got the Blues; Gary Moore's Parisienne Walkways; other beautiful and heartbreaking melodies of the loving and losing humans go through). The solo includes extraordinary virtuosity in its fast parts (shredding is ok) and hauntingly achy arcs and truly heartbreaking moments in its slow sections. These two seamlessly recapitulate each other in the crescendo leading into their resolution at the end. The song is called "I'll Never Love Again" and it's the quintessential loved and lost tune."

## What this song is really about

Everybody who has loved somebody and lost them knows the sentence "I'll never love again". People say it at kitchen tables at two in the morning. They mean it. And it is almost never true in the way they mean it.

Our song lives in that gap. The singer makes a vow, and every bar of music proves the vow is not what he thinks. He will never love again because he never stopped the first time. You cannot start what you have not finished. That is the whole song: one sentence that is a lie and the truth at once.

So the song has two voices, and they do not agree.

The **singer** is a plain man. He does not have the words. He reports small facts and lets them stand. He is holding himself together, and you can hear the effort.

The **guitar** is everything he cannot say. It is the part of him that is not holding together. When the words run out at the end of the bridge, the guitar takes over and tells the truth for almost two minutes: first slowly, like someone who can barely speak; then faster, like someone who cannot stop; and then both at once, when the slow melody comes back an octave higher with all that speed pouring into it. That moment, where the ache and the fury turn out to be the same thing, is the reason the song exists.

Think about what a great slow blues solo does to a person. One note, bent up a whole step and held until the vibrato starts late and wide, can carry more grief than a page of words. The space after a phrase is part of the phrase. A fast run is not decoration: it is what happens when a feeling gets too big for a slow melody to hold. And the best players always come home to the tune. That is the tradition we are writing in. Know it, love it, and then give me something that is ours and nobody else's.

## What I want from you

Bring your whole knowledge of this music: the blues and its descendants, the great ballad players, the great plain-spoken lyricists, the poets who wrote about losing someone and did not flinch. Use all of it as understanding, never as material to copy.

Have taste, and use it. If a line is merely fine, say so, and find the one that is true. If I am wrong, tell me why. If you see a better idea than the one I asked for, show me both. I would rather have one line that stops a listener in the street than ten that scan.

The standard for a lyric line is this: could a real person say it out loud, to someone they trust, without embarrassment, and would the room go quiet? The standard for a guitar phrase is this: would a great player choose to play it slowly, and would it still hurt?

Work as a craftsman as well as an artist. This song has a real score with real constraints, and beauty inside constraints is the job.

## The things that are already right (protect them)

- **Three lines.** "I'll never love again." "I'd have to finish loving you." "I never stopped." These are the spine. They stay.
- **The harmony argues with the vow.** Every time he sings "again", the chord under it is not home. Only the two lines that tell the truth are allowed to resolve.
- **No chorus after the solo.** The guitar has the last big word. The voice gets three lines and the door closes.
- **The melody is fixed for now.** New words must fit the notes that exist: verse lines of 8 syllables in iambic rhythm; chorus 8, 8, 6, 8; bridge 8, 6, 8, 6; coda 8, 6, 4. The build refuses a lyric that does not fit.
- **The guitar part is for a real person.** I will learn this solo on a real Les Paul with 22 frets. Every note must be playable. Virtuosity that a hand cannot reach is not virtuosity.
- **It is ours.** The references teach us architecture, tone and proportion. They do not lend us melodies. No quoting, ever.

## Where I have pushed back (learn my ear)

I threw out two lyric drafts. The reasons matter more than the drafts:

- No clichés, and no stock images: no lights left on, no rain, no ghosts, no flames, no keepsakes on shelves.
- A little poetry, and a lot of plain truth. The pain should be said straight.
- Nothing that sounds like the AI-written verse that fills social media. If a line could appear on a poster, it is dead.
- Too many lines beginning with "I" and "they" made the song small and self-regarding. The fix in draft 3 was to look at *her*: what she did, how she was, and how the house is now.

Draft 3 has not been approved. Read it with fresh ears and tell me honestly what you think of it before you defend it or change it.

## How to begin

Do not write anything new yet. First:

1. Tell me, in your own words, what this song is and what it must make a listener feel in the last thirty seconds.
2. Tell me the strongest thing in what exists, and the weakest.
3. Ask me what you need to know.

Then we work.

## How to talk to me

Write to me in Simplified Technical English at about 80% strictness: short sentences, active voice, one instruction per sentence. Be concise and direct with me. Keep the poetry for the song.

When you change a lyric, show me the whole lyric. When you could not do or could not check something, say so plainly. Nobody in this project except me can hear: the audio is General MIDI with a clarinet for the voice, and everything else has been checked with data. Be honest about that limit, and help me be the ears.
